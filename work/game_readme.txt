NOSTRAVECTOR — ANTARCTICA PROTOTYPE
NV-01: THE WANDERER / JAMES BROWN

OPEN
Open NOSTRAVECTOR_Main_Menu.html in a modern browser with WebGL enabled. The menu, character meshes, complete Earth texture, galaxy intro, five narrator audio clips, and Antarctic sky/snow textures are embedded for offline use. The editable source is included in the ZIP.

START AND CONTINUE
NEW GAME plays the narrated Milky Way intro and opens character customisation. Saving a new Wanderer starts Chapter 01 in Antarctica. James Brown finds you unconscious and delivers five voiced lines using JB1.mp3 through JB5.mp3, with matching captions. Each line waits for its recording to end, then leaves a 1.1-second pause before the next. NEXT or Enter advances manually. Control is released after the fifth line. CONTINUE resumes the selected character's expedition, including partial dialogue playback.

After one minute of active free roam, James says, "I do have a car we can use," using JBIMPATIENT.mp3. The subtitle stays through the recording and a short pause while you keep control. Pausing or opening the map stops voice playback and the timer. The line is saved independently for each Wanderer and is not repeated after it finishes. James voice volume is available in Settings. If autoplay is blocked, ENABLE JAMES VOICE starts the recording. Failed audio retains captions.

POLAR ROVER
James's six-wheel expedition car becomes available beside you with his car suggestion. Walk within six metres and press F or ENTER CAR. James starts as the driver and follows the five-minute automatic route to the station. Press X or TAKE THE WHEEL to switch to manual driving; press X again to let James take over from your current position. Driver switching changes the seated characters and preserves the car's position. James continues the remaining automatic-drive time; a long detour may extend it to keep his speed within the car's limit.

Manual driving: W/Up accelerates, S/Down brakes then reverses, A/D or Left/Right steers, and Space applies the handbrake. Forward speed tops out at 200 mph (89.408 metres per second); reverse is limited to 20 mph. A speedometer shows actual speed and the selected driver. The car accelerates, coasts, brakes, and stops against rocks. You can reach the station sooner by driving yourself. Arrival completes the objective and gets both characters out; you can board again and drive around the sandbox after reaching the station. Drag or Q/E looks around, M opens the map, Escape pauses, and F stops and exits. Small-screen movement buttons also operate the throttle, brake/reverse, and steering, with an on-screen handbrake.

Each Wanderer saves the car's position, heading, driver, speed, steering, distance travelled, automatic route, and trip progress. Save/Continue restores manual driving or James's automatic drive. Older car saves default to James as the driver. Existing saves that already heard the car line receive a parked car.

ANTARCTICA
Free roam across deterministically generated snow terrain, with streamed chunks, scattered ice and rocks, wind-driven snow, storm fog, and the supplied 1024 x 1024 sky and snow images. Each character has a separate terrain seed. The world generates as you explore in any direction. Follow the station bearing, guide poles, or field map toward the research station. James follows the route you walked, catches up, and stops beside you.

The station's approach is 1,680 metres from the starting point. Direct walking at 1.4 m/s takes 20 minutes. Holding sprint takes about 15 minutes INCLUDING stamina recovery: sprint at 2.8 m/s for 30 seconds, then walk for 60 seconds to recover. Detours, stops, or low frame rates extend the journey. Arrival completes the station objective; only SAVE GAME preserves your journey, and free roam remains available. The station has an open doorway and a simple shelter interior.

CONTROLS
WASD / arrow keys: move relative to the camera.
Shift: hold to sprint. The on-screen SPRINT button toggles it.
R / AUTO WALK: toggle forward movement.
Drag the scene / Q and E: turn the camera.
M / MAP: open a field map with you, James, and the station.
F / ENTER CAR: board James's car; while riding, stop and exit.
X / SWITCH DRIVER: swap between James and the player while in the car.
While driving: W/Up gas, S/Down brake/reverse, A/D or Left/Right steer, Space handbrake.
Escape / PAUSE: pause, save, resume, or return to the menu.
Touch movement buttons are available on small screens.

ANIMATION
Both characters use articulated procedural walk/run cycles, with knee bending, foot lift and stance phases, arm counter-swing, body bob/lean, smooth gait changes, and feet adjusted to the local terrain height. These are procedural animations on cartoon models, not motion-capture animations. Footprints mark recent movement. James crouches near the player during the wake-up scene and stands as the conversation progresses.

JAMES'S LIP SYNC AND STATION CONVERSATION
James's twenty-six supplied recordings drive his jaw using decoded audio energy, including silent gaps and saved playback positions. Rounded/wide lips, an oral cavity, teeth, tongue and chin motion accompany the speech. Mouth shapes approximate the transcript; exact phoneme alignment would require phoneme timing marks. Browsers without Web Audio use a timed approximation.
Press T / TALK while within six metres of James and outside the rover. Before reaching the station he plays the supplied reassurance recording. At the station a ten-line conversation begins, with a close-up camera. Enter / NEXT advances each line; movement resumes after the tenth. James discusses exploring the Sol System, surviving Antarctica, checking supplies and staying in the shelter. Conversation progress saves per character; Continue resumes the current line. Completed conversations can be revisited with T.
JBPA1.mp3 through JBPA10.mp3 now play James's ten station lines in order, with recording-driven mouth movement. Pausing stops his recording; Continue resumes the saved line and playback position. Revisited conversations use the same recordings. The new objective is to stay in the shelter, recover and check supplies; sandbox exploration remains available.

CHARACTER CUSTOMISATION
Choose gender, name, skin tone, hair style/colour, eye style/colour, mouth, ears, and spacesuit colours. Face and full-suit cameras, helmet toggle, drag rotation, wheel zoom, presets, randomise, reset, and JSON profile export are included. The player's saved suit colours appear in Antarctica. The expedition helmet stays on.

SAVES AND CHARACTER SLOTS
Expedition progress saves only when you press ESC > SAVE GAME. Timers, pauses, focus changes, closing the page, conversations, checkpoints, hazard recovery and returning to the menu never save progress. RETURN TO MAIN MENU discards changes made after your last manual save; Continue loads that save. The character creation and appearance Save buttons still save the character you explicitly create or edit. Each Wanderer saves their appearance, terrain seed, player position, facing, camera, stamina, James's position, dialogue progress, station discovery, and expedition play time. SWITCH CHARACTER selects an independent saved Wanderer. CREATE NEW CHARACTER starts a fresh character without overwriting existing ones. Editing appearance preserves progress. Existing character-only saves start at the Antarctic opening.

Saves are local to the same browser and origin. Keep using the same URL/port for existing saves. Opening the offline file separately may use a different save store. If browser storage is unavailable, saves last only for the current session. Do not clear browser data if you want to retain your characters.

MANAGING SAVE DATA
Open SWITCH CHARACTER or SETTINGS > MANAGE SAVES & CHARACTERS. SAVE AS creates an independent copy of the current character. REMOVE CHARACTER deletes that character and their progress after confirmation. Removing the active character selects another saved character; removing the last character restores NEW GAME. ERASE ALL SAVE DATA clears this game and its older migration saves without clearing unrelated browser storage. Cancelling changes nothing. Failed storage writes preserve existing saves and display an error. Reset Progress and chapter skipping are removed.

SCOPE AND REBUILD
This is a playable browser prototype with a station objective and open-ended exploration. The supplied snow and sky images are used as textures; terrain is procedural, not a geographic survey of Antarctica. The original astronaut GLB is an importable base template; this app's animations run in JavaScript. The ZIP includes source/work and the base viewer in source/outputs. From the extracted source directory, run Python work/build_menu.py, then work/package_menu.py. JavaScript tests run with Node: test_saved_games.js, test_antarctica.js, test_antarctica_runtime.js, test_antarctica_speech.js, and test_antarctica_hazards.js, and test_weather_cycle.js.

SUPER BLIZZARD AND HAZARD TUTORIAL
After James's first station conversation, the suit announces exactly: WEATHER WARNING! SUPER BLIZZARD INCOMING! Enter / LEARN ABOUT HAZARDS opens five narrator lessons about visibility, suit heat, stamina, the beacon/map and shelter. Captions stay until you press NEXT; the final step restores movement. When you manually save, lesson progress and warning state save per Wanderer and resume through Continue. Exposure and storm time are frozen during conversations, the tutorial, pauses and the map. Existing saves that completed the station conversation receive the tutorial when continued.
The warning uses SUIT.mp3, and the five lessons use NARRATOR1.mp3 through NARRATOR5.mp3. NARRATION / OFF keeps captions. Playback pauses with the game and Continue resumes the saved recording position. ENABLE AUDIO recovers from autoplay restrictions. The original Milky Way narrator recordings are unchanged. NOSTRAVECTOR_Hazard_Script.txt contains the script.
The super blizzard uses 8,000 wind-driven snow particles, pale fog with a range of approximately 8–13 metres, and moving foreground whiteout haze that obscures distant terrain and the sky for 300 seconds of active free-roam play. SUIT HEAT drains from 100 to zero after 180 seconds exposed to this storm, restores at 1.2 points per second inside the station, and at 0.6 points per second in the occupied rover. At zero heat, the suit performs emergency recovery, returning both characters to the station interior with 35 heat. The rover remains where it was parked. The storm timer and heat are included in each character’s manual save. Shelter recovery continues after the storm clears.

RECURRING ANTARCTIC WEATHER AND COASTAL SUPPLIES
After the first taught super blizzard, storms start every ten minutes of active free-roam time: five minutes of super blizzard followed by five minutes of clear weather. The next-blizzard countdown appears during the clear window, and SUIT.mp3 plays when each new storm arrives. Pauses, maps, tutorial screens and conversations freeze the weather clock. Weather phase and remaining time are included only when you manually save.
When the first storm ends, James starts a ten-line conversation about gathering supplies at the nearest coastal depot. The current line resumes from a manual save. JBW.mp3 plays line 1; JBW1.mp3 through JBW9.mp3 play lines 2 through 10. The supplied recordings drive James’s lip sync, pause with the game and resume from the saved playback position when you Continue after a manual save. Captions remain available if playback is muted or unavailable. T / TALK revisits the briefing after you learn the plan. The briefing does not repeat automatically after later storms.
The coastal depot is a fictional location in the generated Antarctic sector, 360 metres north and 40 metres east of the station. The objective and local route point there after James's briefing. Leave the rover and approach within nine metres, then press G / GATHER SUPPLIES for food, sealed water and power cells. The objective then points back to shelter. Collecting supplies never saves automatically.
The persistent Earth minimap labels North America, South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia and Antarctica. Your amber marker stays in Antarctica at the very bottom. This global orientation map shows the Antarctic region rather than surveyed coordinates for procedural terrain. Click it or press M to open the larger atlas, then LOCAL / ANTARCTICA for the station, depot, James and rover.
NOSTRAVECTOR_James_Supply_Script.txt contains the ten new dialogue lines for recording.

EARTH DAY / NIGHT CYCLE
Each Earth cycle lasts exactly 30 minutes of active free roam: 20 minutes of daylight, then 10 minutes of night. Dawn and dusk blend into the daylight segment; sky, fog, snow and character lighting follow the cycle. Clear nights show stars and the moon. The station has warm lighting and the suit provides a soft light nearby at night. The HUD shows the day, Earth clock and time until sunset or sunrise. Pauses, maps, dialogue, tutorials, hidden tabs and the main menu freeze the clock. Walking and either driver advance it once per frame. Only SAVE GAME preserves the time; Continue loads that manual checkpoint. Older saves start at their recorded play time. New characters start at dawn, 06:00. The displayed 24-hour clock is compressed into 20 minutes for 06:00–18:00 and 10 minutes for 18:00–06:00.

BED CONSTRUCTION / SLEEP
After James’s five second-blizzard lines, return inside the research station. Press B or the on-screen button to salvage a frame, insulated pad and fabric from the station, then press B again to construct a visible bed. These bed parts are separate from coastal provisions. Approach the bed and press B / SLEEP UNTIL DAWN. A rest transition, including the first-dream sequence, advances the Earth clock to the next dawn, restores stamina and suit heat, and leaves James with you. Antarctic weather follows its existing ten-minute schedule while the night passes; dawn can still be stormy. Rest can be repeated later. ESC pauses rest and exposes the manual Save Game button. Only manual saves preserve materials, construction, first-rest completion or a rest in progress, separately for each character. Continue restores that saved state; a completed rest returns to visible free roam. There are no autosaves.

DREAM / FERRY / INVENTORY
The first bed rest fades into a five-line dream before dawn. An animated 3D armoured alien based on the supplied reference is labelled ????; their species is not revealed to the player. Their warning begins: “James Brown is not trustworthy. He aims to betray you when you get to the ferry”. The supplied GREK1–GREK5 recordings play with the five matching captions. Recording energy drives the jaw and approximate vowel shapes. NEXT / Enter advances each line after four seconds; WAKE AT DAWN is available on the fifth line. Each line advances 1.25 seconds after its recording ends. Autoplay blocks wait for PLAY VOICE or manual NEXT. A missing recording falls back to captions. ESC pauses the dream; a manual Save Game stores the current line, timer and recording playback position. Completed dreams do not replay on later rests.
At dawn the objective is Find the ferry, exactly 5,000 metres from the wake location. Follow the compass and LOCAL map to the coastal pier. The ferry is surrounded by Southern Ocean water, with a pier and gangway for safe access. Walking into open water is blocked. Approaching the bridge starts James’s betrayal and the ferry escape described below. James follows on foot and can drive the rover to the ferry when selected as driver after dawn. The crossing transitions into the African coastal chapter.
Press I or C, or INVENTORY / CRAFTING, after the first dawn. Opening equipment pauses the world. A starter kit contains three rations, three water, two power cells, six scrap and four fabric. Consume rations/water for stamina or power cells for suit heat. Craft heat packs (one fabric + one power cell), suit repair kits (two scrap + one fabric), or permanent suit insulation (two scrap + two fabric, 30% less storm heat loss). The station and coastal depot each have a single salvage cache; depot salvage adds provisions too. Full gauges do not waste consumables. All inventories, recipes, upgrades, dream progress and ferry discovery belong to the active character, and only explicit Save Game persists them. Older already-rested saves receive the ferry objective and starter kit.

SOL SYSTEM / CHAPTER 03
At the African spaceship, press F or BOARD SPACESHIP. Orion boards if recruited.
SPACE launches into orbit. M opens the Sol chart with 50 destinations and the requested
game categories. Select a destination and SET COURSE for guided travel, or pilot freely
with W/S throttle, A/D yaw, arrows pitch, Q/E vertical thrust, SHIFT boost and SPACE brake.
P toggles guidance; L begins the approach when close. Every world supports landing and
takeoff; giant planets use floating cloud platforms. F boards again near your ship.

On solid worlds, G gathers scrap from new sectors. X repairs the hull using a repair kit
or two scrap. The asteroid belt, Kuiper belt, planet proximity and solar heat can damage
the hull. If disabled, repair or use the emergency tow back to Earth orbit. Charon has
a station: reach its amber airlock and press T to open it, service your ship, and collect
its supply cache. The interior can be explored on foot.

Manual saves preserve flight phase, position, course, hull, visited worlds and Orion
for each character. Nothing saves automatically. ESC > SAVE GAME is still required.

NASA maps are embedded for all eight planets and major moons. Distances/sizes are
compressed for gameplay; landing terrain is generated. Small uncharted moons and distant
dwarf worlds have interpreted surfaces. See NOSTRAVECTOR_Sol_Sources.md for references.

PLANET SURFACE TEXTURES AND SKY
All solid landing worlds reuse the original Antarctica snow and Africa desert photos.
The renderer uploads these to 2048 x 2048 on supported devices, with mipmaps and
anisotropic filtering. Per-world tints remove the original blue/brown cast, and
triplanar projection avoids stretched cliff textures. Multiple scales and relief
lighting add ground detail. Ice worlds favor snow; rocky and volcanic worlds favor sand.
The sky and terrain share one camera basis and field of view. Clouds and stars use
world directions, so turning does not mirror them or jump at the longitude boundary.

LIVING HABITATS / FLORA AND FAUNA
33 species inhabit Earth’s continental woodlands, Antarctica, Africa, Titan, and the under-ice oceans of Europa and Enceladus.
Antarctica has neutral polar bears, penguins, occasional flying petrels and sparse lichen/moss.
Africa has gazelles, predatory lions, flying kites, grasses, succulents and acacia trees.
Each alien world has a distinct anatomy library: Titan has three-legged striders, scythe hunters
and sail gliders; Europa has bell swimmers, diamond mantles and ribbons; Enceladus has radial
wheels, jaw fans and seven-armed swimmers. Their plants have equally distinct forms.
Names, colors, sizes and field notes are generated from the character's seed and stay stable.
These alien ecosystems and Antarctica's polar bears are fictional game content.
Most worlds remain barren. Europa and Enceladus have no surface life. Approach the cyan
shaft east of your ship, then U descends to a seafloor expedition. U returns to the surface.
Orion follows if recruited. You cannot board the surface ship while underwater.
N scans a nearby unrecorded species within 45 m. J opens the species field guide.
V repels nearby wildlife without killing it: 15 stamina cost and an 8-second cooldown.
Predators pursue nearby players; peaceful creatures flee from players and predators.
Ship pads, the Antarctic shelter and the African camp are protected areas. Suit integrity
recovers in safety. Zero integrity triggers emergency recovery without saving.
Manual saves retain the current habitat, integrity and discoveries. Wildlife uses streamed
64 m cells and bounded render/cache limits, with animated limbs, wings and swimming motion.
Enceladus is a new destination (49 total), with white fractured ice and a procedural orbital
appearance. Its surface and ocean habitats are artistic interpretations, with real-world
background from NASA Science: https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/enceladus/

PLANET-WIDE FLIGHT AND FREE LANDING
Board a spaceship and use SPACE / LAUNCH to lift off into atmospheric or airless surface flight. W/S control throttle, A/D turn, arrow keys pitch, E climbs, Q descends, SHIFT boosts, SPACE brakes. Use H / SURFACE ATLAS to choose ANY latitude/longitude on the current world; Earth has Europe, Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Oceania and Antarctica shortcuts. Click anywhere on the map or enter coordinates. FLY TO SELECTED POINT follows a continuous, accelerated path around the sphere. P cancels guidance. L / LAND HERE slows the ship and descends at your CURRENT coordinates. O climbs into space, where M opens the existing Sol chart. L from orbit enters free flight, rather than landing at a fixed site.
All 50 planets, moons and dwarf worlds support their own globe coordinates and free landing sites. The Earth map uses simplified regional boundaries and imagined procedural landing terrain; this is not a geographic simulator. NASA orbital appearances remain in use. Earth regional terrain differs: polar snow, African dunes and greener continental highlands. Ocean and gas/ice giant landing sites use movable floating/cloud exploration decks. Ice-world ocean access shafts move with your chosen landing site. The original African spaceship site (-28, 18) and Antarctic sector (-78, 20) remain accessible; original Charon Station is at (0, 0). Quest state remains intact.
Manual SAVE GAME preserves the globe position, altitude, ongoing route/landing/climb, local position, parked ship, crew, inventory and wildlife discoveries independently per character. Returning to the menu reflects the current world/continent and coordinates. CONTINUE restores your last manual checkpoint; there is no autosave.

Continental woodlands in Europe, Asia, the Americas and Oceania add meadow grass, broadleaf canopies, peaceful highland deer and ridge kites to the scanner.

CHARACTER RENDERING FIX
Surface rendering uses local GPU coordinates to preserve facial details and layered armour at distant landing sites. Curved meshes use correct normal transforms. Menu/customiser outlines adapt to part size so they do not cover small details. Existing coordinates and manual saves remain compatible.

SPRINT ANIMATION
Sprinting uses a 27-degree forward lean, strong bent-elbow shoulder drive, extended strides, high heel recovery and a longer airborne phase. Travel speed and stamina timing are unchanged.

SAGITTARIUS A* STORY HISTORY
Chapter 3 allows peaceful exploration of Grek-controlled Carax and all five fortress floors. Intergalactic event horizons remain locked until Chapter 4 is complete. After the two-year skip, the Catharsis occupy Carax and travel to Sagittarius A* requires completing Hak’s Aridaxx briefing. Early exploration is saved separately and never clears the final assault.

CATHARSIS ENCLAVE HISTORY
Chapter 3 includes one neutral Catharsis planet, a puppet of the Grek Empire. The same saved planet becomes Williams’s rifle investigation destination in Chapter 4. After the two-year skip, the Catharsis are independent and hostile; their earlier puppet treaty ends. Early visits cannot collect the story rifle or trigger its later transmission and ship invasion.
